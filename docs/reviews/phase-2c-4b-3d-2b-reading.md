# Review — Phase 2c-4b step 3d-2b window reading

## Findings

### 1. Medium — the claimed reveal direction is not measured

- **Record:** `docs/decisions/2c-4b-3d-2b-window-reading.md` §5.3, lines 285–296; §14, lines 665–670; §17, lines 748–751.
- **What the record claims:** question 2 is settled as “down or nothing, never up”; the four operation surfaces did not scroll because they could not, while the editor ended at the offset required by the reveal. The coverage table consequently marks obligation (f) PASS on every surface.
- **What the artifacts show:** every success launch samples only after the transition. On the creator, deleter, mover and duplicator, the conflict sample has `section.detail` as a scroller, while the post-reapply sample says `scroller=none` (for example P27 `probe.log:19,30`, P28 `:17,28,32`, P30 `:16,27,31`, and P32 `:16,27,31`). That establishes that the rebuilt content fits after the transition; it does not observe what happened between the pre- and post-transition layouts, and therefore does not establish the reveal's direction. On the editor, the final values do equal `scrollHeight - clientHeight` (for example P24 `probe.log:30,34`), but §5.3 correctly concedes that the clamp and `scrollIntoView({ block: 'nearest' })` produce the same final value. The same indistinguishability applies to the four surfaces whose overflow disappears; `scroller=none` is not evidence that “nothing scrolled.” No request spy, intermediate frame, or pre-reveal/post-layout sample exists.
- **Minimal correction:** change question 2's answer to: final success geometry is measured and the report is visible, but reveal direction is unresolved on all five surfaces. Remove “down or nothing, never up,” remove the direction part of the §14 PASS, add this limitation to §15, and do not call the scheduled direction question settled. To complete the owed reading rather than merely make the record honest, instrument the reveal request or bracket it with measurements that separate layout clamping from the reveal, then retake the affected success cases in both languages.
- **Kind and disk impact:** defect in the decision record/evidence, not an application defect. It does **not** touch what is written to a user's file.

### 2. Medium — obligation (c)'s “still scrolls” half is replaced with a different observation

- **Record:** `docs/decisions/2c-4b-3d-2b-window-reading.md` §9, lines 405–429; §13 F3, lines 603–609; §14, line 662.
- **Source obligation:** `docs/decisions/2c-4b-3d-1-notes.md` §7, line 477 (and “as above” for the other four surfaces) requires both that “a second press still scrolls” and that a person can tell the presses apart.
- **What the record claims:** both halves are settled and obligation (c) is discharged. It answers the first half by saying the second press “still leaves the report in the band” and calls the reveal idempotent.
- **What the artifacts show:** the ten `:twice` launches do prove the second control press occurred and that text, rectangles, `scrollTop`, `scrollHeight`, and `clientHeight` were unchanged (for example P12 `probe.log:40–53`; P21 contains the same first/second equality). They therefore answer the perceptibility half: a person cannot distinguish the presses. They do not show that a second scroll request was issued or honoured. `src/probe.ts:665–680` presses the control and samples only afterwards; it does not spy on `scrollIntoView`. The record itself admits at §9 lines 427–429 that whether the second attempt ran is not observable. “Remained in the band” is not the owed “still scrolls.”
- **Minimal correction:** mark the first half of (c) unresolved in §§9 and 14 and add it to §15. Keep F3's application finding—no visible feedback after the second press—but separate that finding from the missing evidence about a second reveal request. Completing the obligation requires an observable request/intermediate movement, not another comparison of identical final frames.
- **Kind and disk impact:** the overstatement is a record defect. F3 itself is an application feedback defect and **Low is a reasonable grade** because the refusal and reason are already visible before the second press. Neither touches what is written to a user's file.

### 3. Medium — the repeats establish instability, not that neither historical candidate caused the earlier difference

- **Record:** `docs/decisions/2c-4b-3d-2b-window-reading.md` §12 title and conclusion, lines 499–514; the more careful consequence at lines 525–536.
- **What the record claims:** the 17 px discrepancy is “neither of the two candidate causes”; component changes and re-authored fixtures have both been separated and excluded.
- **What the artifacts show:** P27/P51/P53 are the identical `creator-front:es` case and their retained app executables all have digest `84148bbf…`; their conflict panels are 925/908/908 at `probe.log:19`. This is enough—one differing repeat is enough—to establish that conflict-panel height is not deterministic under the variables the harness records. It is also enough to retire a bare 17 px cross-launch difference as evidence of a component or fixture effect. It is **not** enough to prove that a component change or fixture difference made no contribution to the earlier P01–P11 comparison: an uncontrolled 17 px variation can mask or coincide with a real effect. P28/P50/P52 being 741/741/741 does not change that causal limit, and P05 used an earlier, unprovenanced binary.
- **Minimal correction:** replace “neither candidate cause” and “separates both” with “the discrepancy is confounded by demonstrated between-launch instability and cannot distinguish either candidate.” Keep the sound narrower conclusions: panel height is unstable in at least one repeated case, the instability is not universal in these six repeats, and no other verdict in the record relies on conflict-panel height. Three launches per case are adequate for those existence statements, but not for exclusion of causes.
- **Kind and disk impact:** defect in the record's causal conclusion, not an application defect. It does **not** touch what is written to a user's file.

### 4. Low — F5 contradicts the recorded design reason for intentionally using one disjunctive sentence

- **Record:** `docs/decisions/2c-4b-3d-2b-window-reading.md` §7 lines 370–376 and §13 F5 lines 621–624.
- **What the record claims:** the indistinguishable value-change and eligibility-change arms “call for different actions,” making the absent distinction an application observation and raising the question whether 3d-1's fix was delivered.
- **What the artifacts and governing record show:** P12/P14 and P13/P15 do draw byte-identical localized sentences, exactly as stated. But `docs/decisions/2c-4b-3d-1-notes.md` §4.2 lines 339–358 deliberately chose one widened sentence because all collision members refuse the whole reapply and “the recovery offered is identical for all three.” It also explains that causes can overlap, so assigning one reason would require a precedence claim the predicate cannot support. The rendered sentence is a real disjunction and is true for both isolated arms. Thus 3d-1's truthfulness fix is delivered; the record's assertion that the arms call for different actions is unsupported and contradicts its cited design decision.
- **Minimal correction:** remove “those call for different actions” and either remove F5 or retain it only as a neutral observation that the current model intentionally does not attribute a collision to one disjunct. Do not imply that the 3d-1 fix is incomplete without first changing the design decision and the overlapping-cause model.
- **Kind and disk impact:** defect in the record's characterization, not a demonstrated application defect. It does **not** touch what is written to a user's file.

### 6. Observation — F1's grade is defensible, but its stated grading precedent is not

- **Record:** `docs/decisions/2c-4b-3d-2b-window-reading.md` §13 F1, especially lines 567–590.
- **What the record claims:** the selection notice and its Dismiss control are wholly above the visible band on all seven sampled editor successes, but the finding remains Low in part because 3c-2 previously declined to file the geometry.
- **What the artifacts show:** all seven quoted rectangles trace exactly to P24/P25/P34–P37/P45, and P24 `probe.log:32` proves the notice can be brought to `y=44`; the application therefore has an off-screen interactive status control, but not an unreachable one. That limited impact, the block's informational role, and confinement to one surface support Low. An earlier reading's decision not to file it is evidence that the behavior is reproduced and not a 3d-1 regression; it is not an independent reason to cap the present grade after the new fact that the block contains a control.
- **Minimal correction:** retain Low if desired, but ground it in current impact and reachability. Use 3c-2 only for provenance/non-regression, not as grading authority.
- **Kind and disk impact:** F1 is an application layout/visibility defect; the weakness in the rationale is a record defect. Neither touches what is written to a user's file.

## Verified claims and coverage

- Aggressive samples across §§4–12 found the stated `y`, `box`, `clip`, `scroller`, `scrollTop`, `scrollHeight`, and `clientHeight` values in the named launch logs. I found no sampled numeric measurement that could not be traced. The opening promise that every claim names a transcript **line** is looser than the document's actual practice—many tables name only P-number—but each sampled value is recoverable unambiguously from that launch's log.
- `clip=partial` at a printed boundary of 44 or 689 is compatible with “visible.” `clipOf` in `src/probe.ts:527–547` uses unrounded DOM rectangles, while `boxOf` prints rounded values; the logs also show the block remains effectively stationary when the probe asks to scroll it. The record honestly limits this to a sub-pixel edge and lists the rounding bound in §15. This supports closing the old off-screen-report Medium.
- The 23 rows in `launch.sh` match the 23 `runPlan` arms. Collecting the 53 `--- begin` lines covers every case at least once, and P12–P53 provide both-language launches for every per-component §7 content/geometry obligation except the two movement/request questions identified above. Therefore “no coverage gap remains in the 23-row table” is correct in the defined case-launch sense; it must not be expanded into “every requested fact was measured.”
- Five holes is correct: one second `notice.gone` producer plus four non-editor confirmed-reload transitions. None has a case row or driver arm, so none is an existing unlaunched case.
- The third-binary provenance is sound for the comparisons this record actually uses. The retained P12-and-later executables sampled above all digest to `84148bbf…`, and the record does not use P01–P11 geometry as affirmative evidence. The relink breaks digest continuity but does not undermine the same-binary P12–P53 comparisons. The earlier binaries remain unsuitable for affirmative geometry comparison, as the record says.
- No verdict other than §12's discussion uses conflict-panel **height** as its premise. The report visibility verdict uses position and clipping; success geometry uses clipping/final overflow; the first-line constraint has large positional margin. The record is correct on that narrower point.
- All findings in this review are presentation, evidence, or record defects. None is a defect in the byte-preserving save path or in what is written to a user's file.

## Verdict

**NOT READY** — correct the record's two unsupported movement claims and its §14/§15 coverage consequences; replace §12's causal exclusion with the supported confounding conclusion; correct or remove F5's “different actions” characterization; and ground F1's Low grade in current impact rather than prior non-filing. Because success reveal direction and the second-press reveal request were explicit obligations and are not observable in the retained logs, completing 3d-2b also requires targeted instrumentation and retakes for those facts before the reading can claim every §7 obligation is measured.
